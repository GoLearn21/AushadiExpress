Strategy brief · Voice companion for India · Aug 2026

Tolan for Bharat

Tolan’s model does transfer to India — but three of its core choices break on contact. Star voices are the fastest route to a Delhi High Court injunction, monthly subscriptions leak through UPI, and a 35-minute daily voice session costs more per user than an Indian subscriber spends in five years.

Verdict 1 · The voices

Don’t clone the heroes. Cast the archetypes.

Indian courts have protected voice as a personality right in case after case since 2023. Licensed dubbing artists playing film archetypes give you ~80% of the emotional pull at zero legal exposure — and India has roughly 20,000 of them actively seeking these deals.

Verdict 2 · The audience

Women 28–45 in tier-2/3, in their own language.

Adults, so no child-consent regime. Non-romantic, so no obscenity exposure. Already proven to pay for exactly this — someone to talk to about their life — through astrology apps. The highest-demand segment (young men) is the highest-risk one.

Verdict 3 · The shape

A five-minute daily ritual, sold in coins.

Live voice costs roughly ₹3 a minute in Indic languages. At a realistic Indian ARPU you can afford about seven minutes of it per user per month. That single number determines the entire architecture.

The voices

Why the star-voice idea has to change — and what replaces it

This is the part of the plan that would end the company, so it goes first. India is currently the most aggressive jurisdiction on earth for celebrity personality rights, and voice is explicitly covered.

And the labelling rule closes the back door

India’s amended IT Rules took effect 20 February 2026. Any audio that realistically simulates a real person is “Synthetically Generated Information” — it must carry an audible disclosure, embed permanent provenance metadata that you may not allow anyone to strip, and be removable within roughly three hours of a court or government notice. So even a technically legal soundalike has to announce itself, which destroys the illusion you were buying it for.

What to build instead

The emotional pull you saw in that video is not really coming from a specific actor. It is coming from a recognisable relational archetype delivered in a familiar regional cadence — the teasing elder sibling, the unshockable best friend, the grandmother who asks whether you have eaten. Indian cinema built those archetypes; no one owns them.

  • Cast real voice artists, on paper. India has roughly 20,000 dubbing freelancers, and the Association of Voice Artists is actively campaigning for consent, credit and fair pay on exactly these deals. You are the buyer they have been asking for.
  • Get explicit synthetic-voice consent in the contract — scope, term, territory, per-use or revenue-share royalty, and a revocation clause. India has no binding industry AI agreement yet, so your contract is the standard.
  • Make it the marketing. “Every voice in this app belongs to a real artist who is paid every time you hear it” is a defensible, press-friendly position in a market where the story right now is AI replacing voice artists.
  • Never imitate a living person, and put a name-and-likeness filter in front of the model so users cannot coax it into doing an impression on request.
  • A licensed star can come later. No Indian precedent exists for an official AI-likeness deal, so you would be drafting it from scratch, paying heavily, and taking key-person risk. It is a Series A move, not a v1 move.

Why women 28–45 is the beachhead

Every signal you asked me to read — what people watch, what they talk about, what they actually pay for — converges on this group.

  • What they watch. The top Hindi serial for years running is Anupamaa: a homemaker rebuilding her life. The largest TV serial audience segment is 31–50. Micro-dramas — now the fastest-growing format in India, 250M+ app downloads and 16× growth — run on family conflict and emotional betrayal, not superheroes. The market is telling you what this audience is starving for.
  • What they pay for. Astrotalk did ₹1,214 crore in FY25 with ~₹250 crore profit and a ₹2,500 crore run-rate — 90–95% of it from people paying roughly ₹20 a minute to talk to someone about their life for about ten minutes. Users are ~54% female. This is a solved question: Indians already pay, by the minute, for a conversation about their problems.
  • Why they can’t get it elsewhere. India has about one psychiatrist per 150,000 people, and stigma keeps most people away regardless. But 61% of Indian users with anxiety say they are open to AI-based support and 49% say AI actually reduces the stigma. Astrology is the culturally sanctioned wrapper for this conversation; a friendly companion can be the second one.
  • Why voice. 280M Indians use voice search weekly and 62% of Indian voice queries are in Hindi, Hinglish or a regional language — because typing in Indic script is genuinely hard. Voice isn’t a premium feature here, it’s the accessibility layer.
  • Why now. Kuku FM has 10M+ paid users, 70% from tier-2 and beyond, an 11% conversion rate and ₹600 ARPU. The habit of paying for vernacular audio companionship already exists in exactly this geography.
The friction nobody plans for

Phone access for this segment is not private. In one survey 43% of rural Indian women with a household smartphone had not used it in the past month, with family restriction the main reason, and only about 25% of rural women have regular mobile internet against 45% of men.

That is a product requirement, not a footnote: a neutral app icon, notifications that never show content on the lock screen, a PIN or pattern lock on conversation history, and one-tap panic-clear. Design for a phone that other people pick up.

Why the other segments rank where they do

Rank 2 · the margin play

Seniors, billed to their children abroad

Nearly 73% of Indian seniors report emotional neglect as a major concern. The payer is the NRI child, and diaspora ARPU is transformative — Sri Mandir earns about ₹7,000 per user abroad versus ₹600–800 in India, with 20% of revenue from roughly 2.5% of users. Regional OTT shows the same pattern. Launch India and diaspora together, price them differently, and let the diaspora fund the domestic base.

Rank 4 · the trap

Young men are demand you should refuse

India is Character.AI’s second-largest market and 55% of its users are 18–24, so the pull is real. But in January 2026 MeitY gave X a 72-hour ultimatum over obscene AI content from Grok. Rule 3(1)(b) of the IT Rules bars hosting obscene material, and an “AI girlfriend” positioning walks straight into it. Serve men who show up; never market to them; keep romance and sexual content hard-blocked at the model layer.

Rank 5 · the disqualifier

Under-18s are legally out of reach

India’s DPDP regime is the strictest in the world on this: everyone under 18 is a child, requiring verifiable parental consent, with tracking, profiling and targeted advertising outright prohibited. Layer on 14,488 student suicides in 2024 and ~70% of aspirants showing moderate-to-high anxiety, and a crisis-protocol failure becomes existential. Age-gate at 18 and mean it.

The economics

The number that decides everything

Sarvam prices Indic speech at roughly ₹0.05 per second of speech generated and ₹0.04 per second recognised. In a real back-and-forth, about half the clock is each side talking — so a minute of live voice conversation costs around ₹3, before the language model. That is cheap by Western standards and ruinous by Indian ones.

Read it the other way and it becomes a design spec. If you keep 100% of a ₹50 monthly ARPU and spend all of it on inference, you can afford about 16 minutes of live voice per user per month. At a defensible 60% gross margin, you can afford about seven. Roughly fourteen seconds a day.

So the architecture is forced, not chosen

Most of the product must be pre-generated and broadcast; only a thin, precious layer is live. A three-minute daily audio segment synthesised once and served to a million users costs about ₹9 in total, not ₹9 million. Live, personalised, remembering-you conversation is the expensive part — so it is the part you meter and sell.

This is Kuku FM’s economics wrapped around Tolan’s emotional design, and it is the only version of this that survives contact with Indian ARPU.

Coins, not subscriptions

The payment model is not a preference question in India, it is a plumbing question. UPI AutoPay is structurally leaky: roughly 20 million mandate revocations a month on insufficient balance, failure rates several times higher than card mandates, and success decaying toward 70% by month six as balances run short and apps get uninstalled.

  • Pocket FM replaced monthly subscriptions with coins and watched microtransaction revenue grow 484% to ₹934 crore. Coin packs start at ₹49.
  • Astrotalk never sold a subscription at all — wallet top-up, ~₹20 a minute, ~ten-minute sessions, ₹1,214 crore.
  • So: a free daily ritual that costs you almost nothing (cached audio, short text), and coins for live voice minutes, from a ₹49 pack. Sell minutes at ₹5–10 against a ₹3 cost and the margin holds at any usage level. Sell “unlimited” at ₹99/month and your best users bankrupt you.
  • Bill outside the app stores where you legally can, as the Indian incumbents do — a 15–30% platform cut is most of your gross margin at these price points.

The product

What the thing actually is

A non-human companion with a regional voice, one short daily ritual it initiates, a memory that proves itself, and metered live conversation when you want to go deeper. Tolan’s four parts, rebuilt to Indian cost and Indian culture.

The character rules

  • Non-human, and not an alien. The non-human form is doing real work — it suppresses romantic projection and makes the thing shareable. But an alien is an American import. Draw the creature from a regional folk-art visual language instead, so it reads as native and screenshot-worthy at once.
  • Not a deity, not a guru, not a saint. Religious framing monetises brilliantly in India and carries offence risk you cannot insure against. Stay adjacent to warmth, away from worship.
  • Ungendered by default, with the voice chosen at pairing. This is what keeps you out of “AI girlfriend” territory structurally rather than by policy.
  • One character per language, cast properly — not one character with five dubs. A Telugu companion and a Tamil companion should feel like different people, because a Telugu friend and a Tamil friend are.

Speak the spoken language, not the written one

This is the detail that will separate you from every well-funded competitor, and it is a content problem rather than a model problem. Indic text-to-speech trained on written text sounds like a news bulletin. Real people do not talk like that — and in Tamil the gap between literary and spoken registers is so wide they are effectively two languages.

Staff this the way Portola did — their head of story is a published novelist, and non-engineers ship prompt changes straight to production, which bought them a 4× iteration rate. Your version is a writer per language with production access. That is five hires, and it is the moat.

The daily loop

Free · costs you ~₹0

The 3-minute morning segment

Pre-generated once, served to everyone: a short story, a thought, something to think about today, in your language and your companion’s voice. Personalised only by cheap text at the edges. This is the habit, and it is nearly free at any scale.

Free · cheap text

The check-in it starts

It opens the loop, never a blank box. It remembers what you said yesterday and asks about it. Text-first with a short cached voice greeting — the memory callback is what converts, and memory is cheap.

Coins · ~₹3/min cost

Live voice, when you want to talk

Real conversation, metered, from a ₹49 pack. This is the Astrotalk behaviour your users already have. Price it at ₹5–10 a minute and the unit economics hold no matter how heavy the user.

Keep Tolan’s guardrails — they are worth more here than there

Tolan blocks romance and sexual content, flags unhealthy engagement, nudges people toward real life, and suggests signing off after an hour. In the US that is good citizenship. In India, under MeitY’s current posture, it is the difference between a business and a 72-hour compliance notice.

Distribution

How it gets found

Tolan’s sequence holds — find the hook organically, then industrialise it as paid. Only the surfaces and the format change.

  • Android-only for v1. iOS CPI runs 2–3× Android and iOS is a rounding error of the segment you are targeting.
  • Make the creative a micro-drama, not a demo. This is the format India is rewarding right now: 250M+ micro-drama app downloads, 16× year-on-year growth, and in a ShareChat–Kantar study 86% of viewers watching more than fifteen minutes a day with 77% discovering content in social feeds. A 30-second emotional scene — the empty house at 3pm, the thing you cannot say to your husband — will travel. A screen recording will not.
  • Post in-language, natively. Regional-language reels see roughly 30% higher completion, and 79% of Indian users trust a brand more when it speaks their language. One creator team per language, not one campaign translated five ways.
  • Surfaces: Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, ShareChat and Moj. ShareChat and Moj are where tier-2/3 vernacular audiences actually are, and they are far less contested than Instagram.
  • Volume before spend. 30–50 organic videos across 5–10 accounts per language before a rupee of media. If nothing clears 100k views, the hook is wrong — and paid spend on a wrong hook is just a faster way to lose the money.
  • Launch the diaspora simultaneously. Same app, geo-priced. At ~10× ARPU the diaspora may fund your entire Indian user base, and that is the documented pattern across Sri Mandir and regional OTT alike.

Build plan

First ninety days

One language, one segment, one ritual. The instinct to launch five languages at once is the main way this fails — you would be running five content pipelines before knowing whether one works.

Wk 1–3

Pick one language and find the hook in it

Telugu or Marathi over Hindi: less contested, strong vernacular preference, and a real diaspora. Shoot 30–50 micro-drama-shaped clips across 5–10 accounts. In parallel, sign your first three voice artists with explicit synthetic-voice consent.

Gate: one clip past 100k views, and three signed voice contracts.
Wk 4–7

Character, memory, the daily segment

Pairing flow with a real personality quiz — it is the personalisation data, the attachment moment and the shareable artefact at once. Memory as retrieval, not transcript. The cached three-minute morning segment. Spoken-register scripts written by a native writer, not translated.

Gate: a day-2 user is greeted with something they said on day 1, in spoken register.
Wk 8–10

Live voice, metered, behind coins

Sarvam Saaras for recognition and Bulbul for speech, with the whole loop under two seconds. Coin wallet from ₹49, minutes priced at ₹5–10. Instrument cost-per-active-user as a first-class metric from the first day it ships — not after.

Gate: gross margin above 60% on live minutes at real usage, and sub-2s round trip.
Wk 11–13

Compliance, then scale the hook

Age gate, AI disclosure, provenance metadata, crisis escalation, jailbreak testing — all before paid spend, because paid spend is what brings the regulator’s attention. Then restage the winning organic clip as produced paid creative and open the diaspora geo.

Gate: compliance checklist signed off by Indian counsel; CAC below 90-day revenue per user.
Suggested stack

Speech: Sarvam Saaras v3 (recognition) and Bulbul v3 (speech) — it beat ElevenLabs and Cartesia in a blind Indic evaluation across 11 languages and costs roughly an order of magnitude less. Evaluate Sarvam Edge for on-device recognition, which removes your per-query recognition cost entirely and is the single biggest lever on the constraint above. Reasoning: a cheap model for routing and a strong one only for the emotional turn. Payments: a coin wallet on UPI collect, not AutoPay mandates. Quality: a log-review and prompt-iteration tool your writers can use without an engineer.

Risks

What would make me wrong

  • Astrotalk’s users may not be your users. One analysis puts ~85% of its base in metros and tier-1, aged 18–35 — not the tier-2/3 women I am pointing you at. The proof that Indians pay to talk about their lives is solid; the proof that this specific segment does is thinner. Test it in weeks 1–3 before building.
  • Tolan itself has stalled. Estimated traffic down about 35% over three months in 2026, with pricing complaints. Companion novelty decays, and India will decay it faster because switching costs are near zero and free alternatives are everywhere.
  • The cost curve may save you. Every figure here assumes 2026 Indic speech pricing. On-device inference and falling model costs could turn a ₹3 minute into a ₹0.30 minute within eighteen months, which would make the long-session product viable after all. Build metered anyway — you can always give minutes away.
  • Regulation is moving mid-build. The IT amendments landed in February 2026, DPDP consent-manager obligations arrive in November 2026, and a Synthetic Media Bill is in draft. Budget for compliance rework, and get Indian counsel involved in week one rather than week twelve.
  • Distribution may be more expensive than modelled. The ₹25–45 Android CPI is a category average. Emotional-companion creative is unproven at scale in India; assume you pay to learn.
